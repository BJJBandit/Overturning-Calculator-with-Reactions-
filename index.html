Overturning Calculator with Reactions AS/NZS 1170.2:2021

Wind overturning, stability and hold-down point reactions for rectangular buildings and circular tanks, built on the Wind Actions to AS/NZS 1170.2 calculator.

Scope & disclaimer: This is a preliminary/educational tool, not a substitute for the full Standard or engineering judgement. It covers Sections 2–4 (site & design wind speed) and a simplified Section 5 (enclosed rectangular buildings, walls and roof pitch <10° only). It does not cover: Section 6 dynamic response (Cdyn is left as a manual input, default 1.0), Appendices A–E, area reduction/combination/local pressure/porous cladding factors (Ka, Kc, Kl, Kp are taken as 1.0), NZ lee-zone multipliers, C/D coastal-distance interpolation, or pitched roofs ≥10°. Always verify against AS/NZS 1170.2:2021 and have results checked by a suitably qualified engineer. Sections 4–5 add a whole-building overturning check and a rigid-base hold-down distribution — a preliminary hand check that ignores wall friction drag, wind at 45° to the axes, and the flexibility of the base. Sliding, bearing and anchor design are not covered. Full scope notes →

1. Building (plan size, height, roof, orientation)

Describe the building first — its plan dimensions and height are used both for the site-exposure calculation below (average roof height) and for the wall/roof pressure calculation further down.

Ridge runs parallel to W (across the θ=0° wind, Section 2). Section 3 below calculates pressures for all 4 building orthogonal axes automatically, so wind direction relative to the ridge no longer needs to be picked here.

Drag the compass (or use the dropdown above) to set the building's bearing. θ = 0° is the face used as the origin for the design wind speed table below (Clause 2.3: the governing speed in the sector ±45° either side); the shape at centre reflects the selected building type and, where applicable, the plan dimensions (W × L) entered above. Circular shapes have no orthogonal faces, so the θ axes and ±45° sectors are hidden for those types.

Shielding multiplier, Ms (Clause 4.3, Table 4.2)

Ms is forced to 1.0 automatically for h > 25 m. Calculating the shielding parameter (s) from upwind building geometry (Cl 4.3.3) is not automated here — enter s directly if known, otherwise leave blank for the conservative default (no shielding credit, Ms = 1.0).

Topographic multiplier, Mt (Clause 4.4)

Sourcing H and Lu: draw a line along the wind direction, through the site and the upwind crest, in Google Earth (desktop/Pro) and use its elevation profile / path tool — read H as the rise to the crest and Lu as the distance back from the crest to where the profile drops to half of H. Google Earth's terrain is SRTM-derived (~30 m grid) — fine for a preliminary check, but for a site near a significant hill/escarpment prefer an official DEM/contour source (Geoscience Australia's ELVIS, LINZ data in NZ, or a site survey) for the final design value.

3. Design wind pressure – enclosed rectangular building (Section 5, simplified)

Calculates wall and roof pressures for all 4 building orthogonal axes (Clause 2.5.2's minimum), each using its own governing Vdes,θ from Section 2. Walls are fully covered (Tables 5.2(A)–(C)) at every axis. Roofs are covered for all pitches up to 45° when wind is normal to the ridge (front/back axes, Tables 5.3(A)/(B)/(C)); for pitch ≥10° with wind parallel to the ridge (side axes), source a Cp,e value from the Standard directly — see the note on each axis below. Ka, Kc, Kl, Kp are all taken as 1.0 (no area reduction / local cladding factors) — apply these separately for cladding and fixing design.

Plan size (W × L), roof height and roof type/pitch are set in the Building section above.

4. Overturning & stability (whole building, about the leeward base edge)

Sums the wall and roof wind actions for each wind direction into a horizontal force, a roof uplift and an overturning moment, and compares it with the restoring moment from self-weight: γG·W·d/2 ≥ γW·MOT. A rectangular building is checked for all 4 orthogonal axes and both roof load cases (Clause 5.4.1); a circular tank is checked for the governing wind speed from any direction.

Permanent weight only, acting at the plan centre. Use the lightest case.

Defaults are the AS/NZS 1170.0 stability combination (0.9G with Wu) — use an ultimate R in Section 2. For a working-load check with a factor of safety instead, set γG = 1.0 and γW = FoS.

5. Hold-down points

The factored net vertical load and overturning moment are shared between the hold-down points as a rigid base: Ri = N/n + M·si / Σsi², where si is the point's distance downwind of the group centroid. Negative Ri is tension the hold-down must carry.

Applied to each point's hold-down tension when sizing its concrete footing.

Pick either the diameter or the depth — the other is filled in with the minimum that gives the required volume for a round footing.
