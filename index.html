Scope & disclaimer: A preliminary hand-check tool, not a substitute for the Standard or engineering judgement. The tank overturns about its leeward (downwind) base edge: wind drag and roof uplift overturn, self-weight restores. Actions are unfactored and compared against a target factor of safety — an alternative to AS/NZS 1170.0 load factors (e.g. 0.9G with Wu); use one method consistently. Site and design wind speed use the same engine as the Wind Actions to AS/NZS 1170.2 calculator. Confirm anchorage, sliding, bearing and shell buckling with a qualified structural engineer.

5. Vertical base reactions – far (windward) & near (leeward) edges

Modelled as two edge supports one diameter apart. Net vertical N = W − U at the centre; the wind couple shifts reaction to the leeward edge: R = N/2 ± M/b. Positive = compression into the foundation; a negative far-side reaction is net uplift that hold-down anchors must carry.

6. Circular base – n discrete reaction points

Points spaced evenly around the base circle (radius R = b/2), point 1 at the farthest windward position. Lever arm xi = R·cos θi along the wind. Rigid-base linear distribution: Ri = N/n − M·xi / Σxi².

For odd n there is no point at the leeward position (θ = 180°), so the peak compression falls between points. A flexible ring beam or a bolt group with prying will redistribute load — this is a rigid-base idealisation.
